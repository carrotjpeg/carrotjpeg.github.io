pcb keychain workshop
project page · summer 2026

Designed and ran a two-week PCB design workshop from scratch, teaching a mixed group of beginners and experienced mentors how to go from a blank schematic to a fabricated, assembled board. The curriculum ran async recorded sessions alongside live office hours, spread across two weeks of sessions.

Built the beginner project — an LED keychain — as the vehicle for teaching core concepts: resistors, capacitors, LEDs, potentiometers, diodes, transistors, and the 555 timer, each explained through hands-on analogies rather than just datasheets. Wrote the full slide deck and speaker notes, sourced every component from JLCPCB's catalog, and walked students through schematic capture, footprint assignment, and layout in KiCad.

Alongside the teaching materials, finished my own keychain board end to end — schematic, layout, via placement, silkscreen artwork, mounting holes, and resolving DRC errors — then sent it out for fabrication and assembly through JLCPCB.

Also debugged and documented a fix for a known regression in the ImpartGUI KiCad plugin that was breaking for students on newer KiCad versions, so the whole cohort could keep working without losing a session to tooling issues.
